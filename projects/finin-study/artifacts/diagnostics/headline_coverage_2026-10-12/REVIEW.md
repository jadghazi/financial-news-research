The custom skeptical reviewer was actually recalled as `/root/learning_review` for this task. It inspected source news, current selection/preparation code, cache/index manifests, the new audit script, daily counts and summary. It recomputed quantities in memory, without running model inference, training, downloads or writes. Read-only behavior was maintained; runtime inheritance did not enforce read-only sandbox permissions. External literature delegation was unnecessary for this locally answerable counting question.

Verified all 2,111 raw-date CSV counts, 2,107 retained selection matches, 8,428 nested cap checks, exact within-day deduplication, excluded boundary dates, split/year distributions, quantiles, both weighting conventions and unique cache workloads. Source/script/protocol hashes and all 377 protected files matched. No calculation defect found.

Confirmed: cap 16 keeps 6.02% of training headline occurrences but 20.10% on the average date. Cap 64 changes these to 22.45%/69.00%, and validation to 97.70%/98.36%. These measures must not be conflated: large news days dominate headline-weighted totals. Training median is 78, validation 54 and historical test 26; year variation remains strong.

Cap 64 is a defensible bounded diagnostic candidate. Its train/validation union has 99,494 unique IDs; 27,271 are reusable, leaving 72,223 missing IDs for EACH encoder channel. Each missing headline needs text and sentiment encoding, not two distinct headline inputs. The 16-fold attention-matrix area increase is not a 16-fold runtime estimate.

Unsupported conclusions: cap 16 caused weak predictions; cap 64 restores all news; news volume establishes relevance or historically available information; more input must improve forecasting; or a predictive optimum was identified. Test counts are descriptive only. Any future comparison must preserve matched dates, price information, encoder settings, selection rules and controls, and acknowledge reused evaluation dates and coverage shift.
