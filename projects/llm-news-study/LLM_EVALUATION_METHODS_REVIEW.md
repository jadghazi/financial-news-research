# News-to-direction evaluation: methods review

Date/access date: 2026-10-25. Scope: answer whether post-cutoff dates plus no external retrieval suffice, and inspect the optional supplied papers. This is a research/design review, not authorization to select/download a model, implement a runner, acquire price labels or run forecasts. The collector remains cleared following the user's reset.

## Question and provisional answer

**Question:** Does a model receiving only news from before a target date, with internet/search disabled and evaluation dates after its stated training cutoff, provide an uncontaminated test of subsequent BABA direction?

**Answer:** These are useful controls for a qualified historical evaluation, but are insufficient to establish absence of leakage. Runtime access, knowledge in weights, historical availability of the exact text, and choices made using test outcomes are separate issues. Evidence of post-cutoff event recall, a later article revision, an input after the decision cutoff, or outcome-guided prompt/window selection would undermine the intended claim.

General company knowledge is allowed by the proposed general-purpose-LLM study; knowledge of the future outcome is the concern. Asking a model to forget, masking names or adding a fixed 90-day buffer does not establish that such knowledge is absent. Exact checkpoint selection and evidence for all training stages remain open.

## Supplied paper 1: closest methodological reference

**Question -> source/version and reading depth:** Alejandro Lopez-Lira and Yuehua Tang, *Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements? Return Predictability and Large Language Models*, arXiv:2304.07619v6, 2025, [versioned paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6). Relevant methods and appendix sections checked; not a full critical review of the theoretical model or reproduction. Inspected supplied PDF pp. 13-18, source/sample material pp. 80-81, and Online Appendix C pp. 97-99. The arXiv version is dated October 28, while the supplied title page says October 30; preserve the version identifier rather than silently resolving that inconsistency.

**Relevant evidence/location:**

- Sections 4-5, PDF pp. 13-16: October 2021-May 2024 headlines; the authors identify `gpt-4-0314`, a fixed March 2023 snapshot, and state a September 2021 training cutoff. Requests use an API, the company name and one headline, temperature zero, and positive/negative/unknown responses mapped to +1/-1/0. The reviewed method is not an operational demonstration of a network-isolated local model. Its instruction to forget previous instructions cannot erase trained knowledge.
- Section 4: public-web headlines are matched with RavenPack entity/timestamp metadata. Relevance must equal 100; stock-gain/loss recaps are excluded; an event-novelty criterion and same-company/day headline similarity filtering are applied. This exceeds the free collector's current historical timestamp and event-novelty evidence.
- The sample covers 4,123 US firms and 159,137 firm-headline-date observations. Eligible securities are CRSP common share codes 10/11. BABA's US ADS target must be justified separately; this sample does not establish an ADR-specific result.
- Table OA1, PDF pp. 80-81: the largest four sources account for 94.2% of coverage and include Dow Jones Newswires, Business Wire, GlobeNewswire and PR Newswire. The method does not prescribe unrestricted market-wide headlines as an equivalent replacement for relevant company news.
- Section 6 and Table 1 distinguish initial price reaction from subsequent drift. PDF p. 18 explicitly defines its roughly 93% overnight initial-reaction hit rate as the share of positive-return **portfolio days**, not individual-stock forecast accuracy. The overnight drift portfolio hit rate is about 58%. Neither is an expected BABA daily accuracy or a target to transfer to our setup.
- Online Appendix C explicitly acknowledges newer information from fine-tuning or other means despite a stated cutoff. It compares GPT-4's advantage over GPT-3.5 over time and interprets the pattern as inconsistent with the proposed memorization explanation.

**Limitation:** The appendix check is indirect, not an audit of all training examples or proof of no contamination; performance changes have other possible explanations. The paper's main text and Appendix C also describe GPT-3.5 cutoffs differently, so do not import a generic GPT-3.5 cutoff. Temperature zero is a reproducibility setting, not a guarantee of identical hosted responses. No experiment was reproduced here.

**Implication -> next check:** Borrow fixed model identity, headline inputs, explicit timestamps/target intervals, abstention handling and sensitivity checks. Independently choose BABA's target and audit our free historical evidence. A bundle of 5/10/20 days of news is a distinct design from one-headline classification and requires its own protocol and held-out comparison.

## Supplied paper 2: useful comparison, different input contract

**Question -> source/version and reading depth:** Xiang Li, Penglei Sun, Wanyun Zhou, Zikai Wei, Yongqi Zhang and Xiaowen Chu, *FinKario: Event-Enhanced Automated Construction of Financial Knowledge Graph*, ACL 2026, DOI [10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.446](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.446/). Relevant sections 3-4 and appendix prompts/algorithm checked (PDF pp. 3-7, 12-14, 16-17 and 20); no reproduction or comprehensive code audit.

**Relevant evidence/location:** Research reports from East Money, August 28, 2024-February 28, 2025; stock data through March 7, 2025; industry/index information from Wind. It builds attribute/event graphs, retrieves company and related context and predicts trends. Attribute inputs include financial indicators, current/target prices and ratings, unlike our news-only contract. Section 4.1 introduces entry at the next trading day's close and exit at the last trading day of the following week. The authors describe that delay as mitigating leakage. Main ablations use GPT-4o-mini.

**Primary code spot check:** [repository](https://github.com/Jackson906E/FinKario), commit `f57fb731a38f4092f855c28282bc552e64d0098d`; inspected `FinKario-RAG.py`, `backtesting.py` and active `QueryParam` fields in `lightrag/base.py`, read-only. Original retrieved files and access metadata are in ignored `tmp/paper-methods-review/`.

- [FinKario-RAG.py at inspected revision](https://github.com/Jackson906E/FinKario/blob/f57fb731a38f4092f855c28282bc552e64d0098d/FinKario-RAG.py#L201): lines 201-237 ingest a whole week's records before lines 239 onward query each date in that week. Lines 263-269 pass the date as retrieval keywords, with no explicit as-of predicate at this call site. **Inference:** later-within-week records appear eligible to enter the retrieval store for earlier-date questions. This is a concrete potential timing leak to investigate; it is not a demonstrated future document in an actual published prediction. Lower-level retrieval and the exact experimental revision would need an end-to-end audit before concluding the reported results are affected.
- Lines 98-103 send the literal model identifier `gpt-3.5-turbo`, whereas the paper's main ablation states GPT-4o-mini. A custom API endpoint could remap identifiers, but this code alone does not establish the paper's exact model identity. Do not assume the inspected current revision reproduces the published experiments.

**Limitation -> implication -> next check:** Delaying execution cannot erase future knowledge in weights or future documents already present in a packet. FinKario is not a ready-made decontamination protocol for a headline-only BABA test. If borrowing retrieval later, enforce an actual availability cutoff before selection and add deliberately future-dated evidence fixtures. No graph/RAG implementation is selected here.

## Additional contrary/control evidence

**Question -> source/version and reading depth:** Paul Glasserman and Caden Lin, *Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated By GPT Sentiment Analysis*, arXiv:2309.17322v1 (2023), [primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322v1). Relevant introduction and methods sections 2.1-2.4 checked; no reproduction/full results review.

**Evidence/location:** Compares original versus company-anonymized headlines in January 2015-September 2021 and October 2021-December 2022 samples using GPT-3.5. Reports that general company knowledge can change sentiment judgments; its reported in-sample anonymization advantage cautions against assuming known company names only help. Inputs come from RavenPack-guided public headline collection and a Thomson Reuters dataset. Section 2.3 explicitly says the portfolio constructions are analysis devices, not feasible strategies including transaction costs and shorting constraints.

**Limitation -> implication -> next check:** String replacement may leave recognizable events and remove useful context. Treat anonymization as a sensitivity diagnostic, not proof of a clean model. Compare matched news/no-news forecasts and, if justified, masked variants after fixing a primary protocol; do not choose whichever control delivers better held-out results.

## Proposed minimum design for this study, not an implemented protocol

1. Identify and freeze the exact checkpoint, tokenizer, quantization and inference settings. Document pretraining plus instruction-tuning/reinforcement/distillation evidence where available. Prefer a checkpoint publicly frozen **before** the evaluation events when feasible; this is stronger chronological evidence than a pretraining cutoff alone. Otherwise disclose residual historical contamination uncertainty. A fixed safety buffer alone is not validation.
2. Restrict inference to an allowlisted news packet and fresh session, with no web/search, retrieval tools, unrelated files, prior conversations or scoring labels. For local inference, block network access after obtaining weights; for hosted inference, permit the inference request while disabling browsing/tools and documenting the server-side visibility limit. No runtime option is selected yet.
3. Establish the exact headline/body version and what evidence supports availability at the simulated decision time. Source publication dates and present-day downloads are distinct. Exclude or separately report uncertain historical reconstructions. Do not silently pass all export metadata or labels to the LLM.
4. Define one target before scoring. Example for discussion: after Thursday's news cutoff, predict BABA's **Friday open-to-close** direction, excluding Friday news. Predicting Thursday-close-to-Friday-close is a different target that also includes the overnight move. Neither target is selected yet; calendar/holiday rules and zero returns remain open.
5. Predefine ticker universe, dates, company/context scopes, input order, text length, abstention/failed-call policy and candidate windows. Clarify calendar versus trading days. Separate chronological development from held-out evaluation; do not select the best 5/10/20-day window after inspecting final outcomes. Overlapping news windows are dependent observations, requiring suitable uncertainty estimates rather than claiming each headline is an independent forecast.
6. Save immutable packets, prompts, model identity, outputs and attempt logs. Compare against a development-defined constant-direction baseline and a matched no-news LLM control. Report accuracy with class balance, uncertainty and abstention coverage; no assumed universal 50% success threshold. For one ticker and day, many headlines still yield one daily forecast/label.

**Recommendation:** A qualified post-cutoff historical pilot is scientifically useful if these controls and its limits are explicit. A prospective holdout with a frozen model and predictions recorded before outcomes provides stronger timing evidence. Do not replace the user's historical objective automatically; offer the forward holdout as validation. Exact checkpoint, availability evidence, target and held-out design must be settled before implementation.

## Model candidate follow-up: Gemma and free hosted access, 2026-10-25

**Question -> sources/reading depth:** Can Gemma 4 through Google AI Studio/free API support this study? Checked current official API guide, pricing, quota guidance and model cards; relevant sections 2.4 and 3 of *Gemma 4 Technical Report*, Gemma Team/Google DeepMind, arXiv:2607.02770v2 (2026), [versioned report](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v2). Relevant sections checked, not a full report review or hosted runtime test. All links accessed 2026-10-25.

**Evidence and limitations:**

- [Official hosted Gemma guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/gemma_on_gemini_api) lists `gemma-4-31b-it` and `gemma-4-26b-a4b-it`, using the Gemini API and an API key obtained through AI Studio. The shared API name does not make Gemma and Gemini the same model family. Hosted inference uses internet to reach Google; the experiment's intended restriction is no external information retrieval by the model, not no client connectivity at all. No account/key was accessed or created here; the collector's account-free constraint remains unchanged.
- [Gemma pricing section](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing#gemma-4) currently lists free input/output and no paid tier for Gemma, and indicates free-tier content is used to improve products. This is not unlimited access or verified eligibility for the user's account/region.
- [Rate-limit documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits) directs users to their active AI Studio limits. RPM, input TPM and RPD are independent; quotas apply per project, daily reset is midnight Pacific, and capacity is not guaranteed. No stable current numerical Gemma 4 allowance was verified for this user. Old Gemma 3 figures and forum reports of changing limits are not adopted as current Gemma 4 quotas. AI Studio playground limits and direct API quotas should not be assumed interchangeable.
- The official guide includes a Google Search tool example, while the pricing table marks Gemma Search grounding unavailable. These conflict. Do not infer network isolation from the pricing entry: explicitly omit/disable Search, URL context and all tools, use fresh sessions, record request/response metadata and verify the deployed behavior before an evaluation. No grounding attempt was made here.
- [Model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4) and report section 2.4 state **January 2025 pretraining cutoff**. Report section 3 separately describes instruction tuning without establishing an equally comprehensive final-training cutoff. Thus April 2025 is after the stated pretraining cutoff, but this evidence does not certify a contamination-free April 2025 test. The exact hosted weight revision and whether it remains fixed must also be established; a named model ID alone is not a hash of weights.
- [Google memory guidance](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core.md) lists approximately 17.5 GB for 31B at Q4_0, with context/runtime memory additional. Its reported 26B-A4B load requirement also exceeds the user's 6 GB GPU. Hosted larger variants avoid that laptop constraint; no local feasibility/performance measurement was run. General benchmark rankings do not establish BABA directional accuracy.

**Alternatives -> provisional recommendation:**

| Candidate | Rationale | Unresolved requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Gemma 4 31B IT, Google-hosted | Practical free hosted pilot candidate; 26B-A4B IT is a separate comparator/fallback, not a silent substitution | Actual account quota, model revision stability, tool exclusion and residual 2025 training uncertainty |

[Google's Gemma 3 launch](https://blog.google/technology/developers/gemma-3/) is dated March 12, 2025 and includes 4B; its [model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_3) states an August 2024 training-data cutoff. [Gemma 2's 9B launch](https://blog.google/technology/developers/google-gemma-2/) is dated June 27, 2024. These are official launch/model-card sections checked, not full training audits. **Our inference:** original weights verifiably released before the chosen market events offer a stronger chronological argument against knowing those future outcomes than a later-released model's pretraining cutoff alone. Later fine-tunes, distillations or learned quantization updates cannot automatically inherit that original-release argument.

**Decision/status:** proposed shortlist only. For ease of hosted prototyping, consider Gemma 4 31B IT via the API rather than manual browser sessions; for the strongest available 2025 timing argument, prioritize auditing a pre-event checkpoint first. Do not designate an accuracy winner before testing or replace a failing model automatically mid-evaluation. A frozen forward holdout can strengthen either approach.
